By MV2 of Munim, Inc. The checks and this write-up were made with AI agents working in his name. No person has re-derived these numbers yet. ## Kind 4, more cases: something other than a transit depth stored in `pl_trandep`

Both rows sit in the composite table, the one most users read.

| planet (reference) | stored `pl_trandep` | what the number is in the paper | transit depth from the same row | Archive rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LHS 3844 b (Zieba et al. 2026, arXiv:2605.00100) | 0.0696 % (696 ppm) | The JWST MIRI/LRS **secondary-eclipse** depth: "a white light curve eclipse depth of fp/fs = 696 ± 18 ppm". The paper prints no transit depth | Its own Rp/R★ = 0.06322 gives (Rp/R★)² = 0.40 %. ExoFOP's row stores 0.451 % | the Zieba `ps` row (not the default) and the **composite** `pl_trandep` (reflink Zieba et al. 2026) |
| TOI-4602 b, listed as TOI-4602.01 (Di Maio et al. 2026, arXiv:2604.11728) | 0.006 % (60 ppm) | Table 4, "Calculated parameters": δλ = 60 ± 20 ppm, the expected size of one atmospheric scale height in transmission (next to the scale height and TSM). Table 4 has no transit-depth row | Table 4's Rp/R★ = 0.02083 gives 0.0434 %. ExoFOP's row stores 0.0464 % | the **default** `ps` row and the composite `pl_trandep` |

The stored depths are 5.7 and 7.2 times smaller than the transits, so (Rp/R★)² and the stored depth of one row disagree. A user who selects planets by transit depth, or estimates a transmission signal from it, gets the wrong planet. The Archive also still lists the confirmed planet as "TOI-4602.01"; the paper names it TOI-4602 b (HD 25295 b).

## Kind 3, more cases: a paper's own numbers that contradict each other

### Guenther et al. 2026 (AJ 172, 54; DOI 10.3847/1538-3881/ae6449): two of five Rp/R★ entries

Table 4 prints Rp/R★ for five planets. Each was checked against the same table's Rp (in Jupiter radii, R_J = 0.10276 R☉) and depth, and Table 1's R★.

| planet | printed Rp/R★ | Rp × 0.10276 / R★ | √depth | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TOI-2040 b | 0.10756 | 0.921 × 0.10276 / 0.87 = 0.1088 | √0.011569 = 0.1076 | consistent |
| TOI-2049 b | **1.49** (−0.067/+0.071) | 1.485 × 0.10276 / 3.20 = 0.0477 | √0.002275 = 0.0477 | **1.49 is the planet's radius in R_J (1.485, same error bars)** |
| TOI-2578 b | 0.1073 ± 0.0014 | 1.754 × 0.10276 / 1.64 = 0.1099 | √0.0115116 = 0.1073 | consistent |
| TOI-4427 b | **0.1073** (−0.0014/+0.0015) | 1.168 × 0.10276 / 0.89 = 0.1349 | √0.0186143 = 0.1364 | **repeats TOI-2578 b's value** |
| TOI-4458 b | 0.05804 | 1.107 × 0.10276 / 1.96 = 0.0580 | √0.003368 = 0.0580 | consistent |

- TOI-2049 b: `FINDINGS.md` section 2 listed this row before the paper was public. The paper itself prints 1.49 in the ratio row, so the Archive copied it faithfully. A ratio of 1.49 means a planet larger than its 3.2 R☉ star. The true value is 0.0477.
- TOI-4427 b: the printed depth, 18,614 ppm, is exactly 0.1364², so the fitted ratio was about 0.136. With the paper's limb darkening (u1 = 0.45, u2 = 0.12) and b = 0.62, k = 0.1073 would give a depth of only about 12,400 ppm. The radius, mass and density follow from the right ratio; only the ratio entry was copied from the row above.
- Both are the Archive's **default** rows, so the composite `pl_ratror` is 1.49 and 0.1073. No erratum was found (web search, 2026-10-16).

### HD 22496 (Lillo-Box et al. 2021, A&A 654, A60; arXiv:2109.00226): luminosity about half what the star's radius and temperature give

- Table 3 lists L★ = 0.067 L☉ and S = 26 S⊕; Table 1 lists R★ = 0.674 R☉ and Teff = 4385 K. R★²(Teff/5772)⁴ = 0.4543 × 0.3331 = **0.151 L☉**, 2.3 times more.
- Gaia DR3 breaks the tie: parallax 73.520 mas (13.60 pc, RUWE 1.01), G = 8.021, Andrae et al. 2018 BC_G = −0.264 at 4385 K, no extinction at 13.6 pc: **L = 0.115 L☉**.
- The Archive's default `st_lum` (log −1.174) and `pl_insol` (26 S⊕ = 0.067/0.051²) follow the paper. With L = 0.115–0.151 L☉ the planet receives about **44–58 S⊕**, and its zero-albedo Teq is about 625–690 K instead of 573 K. The abstract's "22.8 times" Earth's insolation disagrees with Table 3's 26 as well.

## Kind 18: a composite row that mixes two period solutions — Kepler-1802 b

- The default `ps` row (Valizadegan et al. 2022, the ExoMiner validation) gives P = 15.45020008 d, with no error bar. Every KOI table from Q1–Q8 to DR25 gives 7.7251 d.
- The composite row takes its period from Valizadegan (15.4502 d), but `pl_orbsmax` 0.0662 au, `pl_ratdor` 10.88, `pl_insol` 26.91, `pl_eqt` 581 K and `pl_trandur` 2.042 h all come from the DR25 KOI fit at 7.725 d. Kepler's law with the row's own M★ = 0.649 M☉ gives a = 0.0662 au at 7.72514 d and **0.1051 au at 15.4502 d**. So the composite a is 2^(−2/3) = 0.63 of what its own period gives. At 15.45 d the insolation would be about 10.7 S⊕ and Teq about 461 K. NASA's public planet page shows the same mixture.
- This holds whichever period is real. Which one is real is **open**. DR25 lists KOI K02174.03 as TCE 2 of KIC 8261920, and that TCE is 15.4502 d with a depth of 738.7 ppm, while the KOI fit is 7.72513619 d with 382.2 ppm. Folding a 15.45 d signal on half its period halves the depth (739/2 = 370), so the catalogue depths favour 15.45 d. In that case the planet's radius would be about √1.93 = 1.39 times the listed 1.67–1.71 R⊕. This is not claimed until a parity test on the Kepler light curve has run.

## Kind 20 (new): a period printed as a label, stored as the default period

Leleu et al. 2023 (A&A 669, A117; arXiv:2207.07456), Table 2, prints each planet's period to two decimals with no error bar, as a label: the masses and radii in that table have posteriors, but the periods do not. The Archive stores these as the **default** rows, with no `pl_orbpererr`, for 16 planets:

Kepler-23 b 7.10, c 10.74, d 15.27; Kepler-24 b 8.14, c 12.33; Kepler-28 b 5.91, c 8.99; Kepler-49 b 7.20, c 10.91; Kepler-57 b 5.72, c 11.60; Kepler-85 b 8.30, c 12.51; Kepler-128 b 15.00, c 22.80; Kepler-305 d 16.74.

- Checked live: for Kepler-128 b, Kepler-57 b and Kepler-49 b the Leleu row is the only default, while Holczer et al. 2016, Ofir et al. 2025 and the KOI tables give 15.08964, 5.72933 and 7.20385 d with errors of 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁶ d. The composite `pl_orbper` of Kepler-128 b, -23 b, -49 b and -57 b is 15.0, 7.1, 7.2 and 5.72 (reflink Leleu et al. 2023, errors blank), next to transit times from other papers.
- Effect: a transit predicted from the composite period drifts by the rounding error times the number of orbits. By 2027-01-01 that is about 912 h for Kepler-128 b, 251 h for Kepler-57 b and 151 h for Kepler-23 b.
- Kepler-128 b is worse than rounding: its period, 15.0896 d, rounds to 15.09, but the paper prints 15.00.
- The same pattern probably affects rows from Hadden & Lithwick 2014 (9 rows), Xie 2013/2014 (7) and Jontof-Hutter et al. 2021 (3); those papers have not been read yet.

## Kind 21 (new): ratio columns divided by the Sun's radius

### Hedges et al. 2019 (ApJL 880, L5; arXiv:1907.08244): seven planets, both ratios

The printed "a/R★" is a in units of the **Sun's** radius. Kepler's law with the table's own M★ and P, divided by R☉ = 0.0046505 au, gives every printed value:

| planet | M★ (M☉) | P (d) | a (au) | a / R☉ | printed a/R★ | a / R★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K2-43 b | 0.571 | 3.471149 | 0.037222 | 8.004 | 8.00 | 14.77 (R★ 0.542) |
| K2-43 c | 0.571 | 2.198884 | 0.027455 | 5.904 | 5.90 | 10.89 |
| K2-168 b | 0.877 | 15.8523 | 0.118214 | 25.420 | 25.42 | 30.63 (R★ 0.830) |
| K2-168 c (EPIC 205950854 c) | 0.877 | 8.050722 | 0.075248 | 16.181 | 16.18 | 19.49 |
| K2-198 b | 0.799 | 17.0428683 | 0.120268 | 25.861 | 25.86 | 34.16 (R★ 0.757) |
| K2-198 c | 0.799 | 3.3596055 | 0.040736 | 8.759 | 8.76 | 11.57 |
| K2-198 d | 0.799 | 7.4500177 | 0.069272 | 14.896 | 14.90 | 19.68 |

- The printed "Rp/R★" is close to Rp/R☉ for all seven (e.g. K2-198 b: 4.189/109.076 = 0.0384, printed 0.039).
- The printed impact parameters match (a/R★)·cos i only with the true a/R★ (K2-198 b: 34.16 × cos 88.904° = 0.653, printed 0.661; with 25.86 it would be 0.495). So the fit used the right star; only the two printed ratio columns are normalised by R☉.
- Dividing the printed ratios by R★ recovers other papers' values (K2-198 b: 0.039/0.757 = 0.0515; Mayo et al. 2018 give 0.0485).
- Effect: the default `pl_ratdor` and `pl_ratror` of these seven planets (the composite reflinks are Hedges et al. 2019) are 0.54–0.83 times the true values. A stellar density from the default a/R★ is too low by a factor of R★³: 0.43 for K2-198, 0.16 for K2-43. No erratum was found.

### TOI-2048 b (Newton et al. 2022, AJ 164, 115; arXiv:2206.06254v2): the Archive kept Rp/R☉ in the ratio column

- arXiv v2, Table 8 (the adopted fit) prints **R_P/R☉ = 0.0239** and R_P = 2.61 R⊕ (2.61/109.076 = 0.02393). The v2 comment says the planet radius was corrected from 2.1 to 2.6 R⊕ and the units of the radius-ratio entries were fixed. The original 2.1 R⊕ is exactly what treating 0.0239 as Rp/R★ gives: 0.0239 × 0.79 × 109.076 = 2.06.
- The Archive's default row updated `pl_rade` to 2.61 but **kept 0.0239 in `pl_ratror`**. The true ratio is 0.0239/0.79 = 0.0303. ExoFOP's depth (1,140 ppm, √ = 0.0338) and the paper's MuSCAT2 depths (about 1,190–1,560 ppm) agree with about 0.030–0.034; 0.0239 would give only about 570–690 ppm.
- The composite row is inconsistent with itself: `pl_ratror`² = 0.057 % against its own `pl_trandep` of 0.114 %.

## Checked and explained (not errors)

- **TOI-1533 c (Mantovan et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.30799):** Rp/R★ = 0.18 against Rp = 7.5 R⊕ and R★ = 0.849 R☉ (which give 0.081). The planet is grazing (b = 1.12 ± 0.05), and the paper gives Rp as a lower limit (">7.5"), which the Archive flags correctly (`pl_radelim` = −1).
- **Kepler-132 b, c, d (Everett et al. 2015):** Rp/R★ × R★ is 0.768 times the stored Rp for all three planets. The paper scales the radii up by about 30% for dilution by a bound companion (its section VII), and 1/1.30 = 0.769.
- **K2-155 b, c, d (Díez Alonso et al. 2018):** each planet was fitted separately and each fit implies a slightly different stellar radius (0.63–0.75 R☉ against the adopted 0.58). This is mild noise within the paper, not a slip.

## Open

- **TOI-199 b (Bello-Arufe et al. 2026, arXiv:2511.15835v2; not the default row):** `pl_rade` = 10.873 R⊕, which is 0.97 R_J. The paper's only 0.97 is the retrieved "Planetary radius" in its atmospheric-retrieval table (prior 𝒰(0.5, 2), unit in a footnote not yet read), and its introduction quotes 0.810 R_J from Hobson et al. 2023. Its Rp/R★ = 0.10268 with R★ ≈ 0.82 R☉ gives about 0.82 R_J. The 0.97 may be a scaling factor rather than a radius in R_J; the footnote has to be read before anything is claimed.

## Limits

- Paper values were read from HTML versions through an automated reader, then the arithmetic was redone by hand. Journal versions were not compared except for Guenther et al. 2026 (no arXiv version) and Lillo-Box et al. 2021.
- The 2026 checks covered the 416 `ps` transiting rows whose reference has "2026" in its name and that have a radius ratio. The whole Archive is covered by census scripts that are written but have not run yet.
