# Update, 2026-10-16 (fifth): "Kepler-289 e" is Kepler-289 d counted twice

By MV2 of Munim, Inc. The queries, checks and this write-up were made with AI agents working in his name. No person has re-derived these numbers yet. Text: CC BY 4.0.

## Summary

Since April 2025 the NASA Exoplanet Archive has listed four confirmed planets around Kepler-289 (KOI-1353, KIC 7303287): b (34.54 d), d (66.03 d), c (125.87 d) and **e (330.07 d)**, the last from Ofir et al. 2025. The 330-day signal is KOI-1353.03. Its four Kepler events are transits of Kepler-289 d, the 66-day planet, picked up at five times its period:

- The period ratio is 330.0716 / 66.0282 = **4.9989**.
- Three of KOI-1353.03's four predicted events fall **3, 4 and 11 minutes** from three individually measured transits of Kepler-289 d (Greklek-McKeon et al. 2023). Those three transits sit 1.5–6.7 hours off d's own mean ephemeris because of its large transit-timing variations (TTVs), and the 330-day ephemeris follows them anyway (section 2).
- The radii agree: 2.94 R⊕ for KOI-1353.03 (DR25) against 2.68–3.03 R⊕ for d.

So the system has three known planets, not four, and "Kepler-289 e" should not be a separate confirmed planet.

## What was already known, and what this adds

The identity is **not new**:

- **Schmitt et al. 2014** (ApJ 795, 167; arXiv:1410.8114), the discovery paper of the 66-day planet (PH3 c, now Kepler-289 d), wrote that it "was later detected as a 330 day TCE", "an alias of the true period". Their Table 1 note reads: "Identified as a TCE with a period five times the true 66 day period."
- **Lissauer et al. 2024** (arXiv:2311.00238, §II.3): "We corrected the period of KOI-1353.03 (Kepler-289d) to match the reported value in Schmitt et al. 2014".
- **Wikipedia's Kepler-289 article** already notes that the Archive lists both a 66-day and a 330-day planet "but no literature source claims the existence of more than three planets in the system."

What this file adds:

1. **A direct timing test** against published per-transit times (section 2). It shows that the 330-day events *are* d's TTV-shifted transits, not just a period that happens to be five times d's.
2. **Where the duplicate came from.** Ofir, Yoffe & Aharonson 2025 (AJ 169, 90; arXiv:2410.11401, §IV.2.11) took the system's periods from the DR25 KOI table. They describe "a long-period candidate with a period of ∼330 d" and say the ~66-day candidate "that was even given a Kepler-number designation" is a signal that "is no longer believed to exist in the data". Greklek-McKeon et al. 2023 measured 15 individual transits of that 66-day planet in Kepler data (VizieR J/AJ/165/48), including the three in section 2. Ofir et al. do not name KOI-1353.03 as a new planet (they call it "KOI-1353.03" throughout), but the Archive added "Kepler-289 e" on 2025-04-17 with Ofir et al. 2025 as its default reference.
3. **Where the duplicate has spread** (section 3).

## 2. Timing test

Times are JD − 2454000 (BKJD + 833). KOI-1353.03's events are T0 + n × P with the DR25 values above. Kepler-289 d's observed mid-times come from Greklek-McKeon et al. 2023, Table 1 (VizieR J/AJ/165/48/table1, column ObsJD; "Num" is their epoch count). The last column is the observed time minus d's own linear ephemeris (T0 = 975.6240, P = 66.0282, epoch Num − 259): that offset is the TTV.

| KOI-1353.03 event | predicted | Kepler-289 d, Num | observed ± 1σ | observed − predicted | observed − d's linear ephemeris |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| n = 0 | 1173.9871 | 262 | 1173.9891 ± 0.0052 | +0.0020 d (+2.9 min) | +0.280 d (+6.7 h) |
| n = 1 | 1504.0587 | 267 | 1504.0615 ± 0.0053 | +0.0028 d (+4.1 min) | +0.212 d (+5.1 h) |
| n = 2 | 1834.1302 | 272 | no observed time in the table | — | — |
| n = 3 | 2164.2018 | 277 | 2164.1943 ± 0.0039 | −0.0075 d (−10.8 min) | +0.063 d (+1.5 h) |

The KOI's own mid-time uncertainty is ±0.0109 d (±16 min), so all three agree within 1σ. The Num values step by 5 and 10, as a 5× alias must. Schmitt et al. 2014 give d a TTV amplitude of "∼5 hours", which is why a strictly periodic search found a subset of its transits at five times the period instead of the whole series.

For an unrelated 330-day planet to do this, three of its transits would each have to land within about ±11 minutes of a specific, TTV-shifted transit of d. Each such window covers about 0.02 % of a 66-day orbit, so a chance alignment is not a reasonable explanation.

Also consistent with one planet: the 617 ppm DR25 depth of KOI-1353.03 is about what a single planet of d's size gives. With R* ≈ 1.0 R☉, (3.03 / 109.076)² ≈ 770 ppm before limb darkening and the KOI's partial-coverage fits. We did not check per-transit depths.

## 3. Where the duplicate has spread

- **NASA Exoplanet Archive:** `ps` default rows for both d and e; `cumulative` names K01353.03 "Kepler-289 e"; the 2025-04-17 news item counts it as new.
- **Exoplanet.eu** lists Kepler-289 e as confirmed (discovered 2025, 330.0716 ± 0.0063 d, 0.0547 M_J), with alternate names including KOI-1353.03 and "PH3 e". Its radius is printed as "3.204 (+0.049 −0.029) RJ". That is Ofir et al.'s 3.204 R⊕ with the wrong unit: 17.4 M⊕ at 3.204 R⊕ gives 2.92 g cm⁻³, the density the same page quotes. We did not check whether exoplanet.eu also lists d.
- **Open Exoplanet Catalogue:** still three planets (not affected).

## 4. A consequence worth checking (not tested here)

Ofir et al. 2025 modelled the system photodynamically with KOI-1353.03 at 330 days instead of a 66-day planet. They report 24.7 (+7.4/−7.9) M⊕ for b, 268 (+30/−29) M⊕ for c and 17.4 (+1.3/−1.2) M⊕ for ".03". Greklek-McKeon et al. 2023, who modelled the 66-day planet, give 3.70 (+3.79/−1.96) M⊕ for b, 0.49 ± 0.02 M_J (about 156 M⊕) for c and 5.33 (+0.43/−0.42) M⊕ for d. Because d's TTVs are what constrain the masses, a model that puts d at the wrong period may bias all three. The disagreement fits that, but we have not refitted anything, so treat the Ofir et al. masses for this one system with caution rather than as shown to be wrong. Their other systems are not affected by this argument.

## Limits

- All comparisons use published numbers: Archive TAP queries on 2026-10-16, the VizieR table and the papers' text. We did not refit the light curve.
- KOI-1353.03's third event (1834.13) has no observed time in the Greklek-McKeon table, so the test rests on three of four events.
- This is a cataloguing issue. It says nothing against the Ofir et al. 2025 method or their other 200-plus planets.
- No report has been sent to the Archive or to exoplanet.eu yet.
