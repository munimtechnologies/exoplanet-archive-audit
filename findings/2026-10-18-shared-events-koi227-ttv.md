# Shared events, sub-type (c): K00227.01's drifting ephemerides are its published ~10 h transit timing variations

By MV2 of Munim, Inc., 2026-10-18. Made with AI agents working in his name; no person has checked it yet.
Text CC BY 4.0. Annotates `findings/2026-10-18-shared-events-results.md`, where K00227.01 was listed under
sub-type (c) ("same signal, ephemerides drift apart") and marked "not examined".

The rows: Archive (Q1-Q16-era fit) 17.6574 d; DR25 TCE 17.7094 d (Robovetter FP, LPP_DV); Lissauer et al.
2024 17.6895 d. The two catalogue periods differ by 0.052 d, or 0.29 %. Over the about 83 cycles in Q1-Q17
that amounts to about 4.3 d, so the two ephemerides agree only in mid-mission. In the shared-events job they
shared 15 of 83 events (p = 7.0e-6).

**Prior art.** Nesvorný, Kipping, Terrell and Feroz 2014 (ApJ 790, 31; arXiv 1405.2060) analysed KOI-227
photodynamically. They found transit timing variations of KOI-227.01 with an amplitude of about 10 hours,
which fit planetary companions in several mean-motion resonances (2:1 outer favoured by the Bayesian
evidence, 3:2 solutions more physically plausible). They did not settle which. Later work uses KOI-227 as a
standard strong-TTV test case (2017 AAS abstract 229.245.13; the RIVERS paper, arXiv 2111.06825). Within a
shorter or differently weighted stretch of data, a linear ephemeris fitted to a train with 10 h TTVs can pick
up a noticeably different mean period. A 0.3 % difference between the Q1-Q16 and DR25 fits, with Lissauer's
full-mission value between them, is what that would look like. We have not refitted the times ourselves.

**Consequence for the census.** Two of the three sub-type (c) cases are now explained by published timing
variations: K00682.01, as noted before, and K00227.01. The third, K05365.01, is a rogue re-detection with all
three events within 1.1 d. None of the three is a period disagreement of the KOI-1356 kind. Read as "the same
signal", they reduce the census's "unrelated" count further, as the results note already said for K00682.01
and K05365.01.
